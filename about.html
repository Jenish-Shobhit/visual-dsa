How a lesson works

Watch, predict, play, check

Every lesson has the same rhythm, so you always know what to do next. Every figure steps backward as well as forward, and every step comes with a caption that says why.

  1. Watch

    A short animation shows the idea in motion: items keep their identity and slide to their new places.

  2. Predict

    Before a step runs, say what will happen. Being wrong here is where the learning is.

  3. Play

    Labs highlight the running line of code, show live variables, and take your own input: presets, random, worst case.

  4. Check

    Quick quizzes and practice problems, with explanations. Solutions to practice are yours to find.

Sources and credits

Where the ideas come from

Reading that shaped the teaching

  • Charles Petzold, Code: the path from physical state to symbolic instruction.
  • Remzi and Andrea Arpaci-Dusseau, Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces: memory and machine models.
  • Robert C. Martin, We, Programmers: the people and history behind the ideas.

These informed the pedagogy only. No text or figures were copied.

What is original

The lessons, traces and figures were written for Visual DSA. The deep studies are the author’s own notes, adapted for the web. Standard algorithms and facts are explained in fresh prose; dates and attributions are checked against primary sources where possible.

Details, and the deliberate teaching simplifications, are in the source notes.

Accessibility, privacy and licence

Built to be used by everyone

Accessibility

  • Everything works from the keyboard: arrows step, Space plays, Home and End jump.
  • Steps are announced through live captions; figures have text alternatives.
  • Light and dark themes, 4.5:1 text contrast, and it works at phone width.
  • Animations are switched off if your system asks for reduced motion.

Privacy

No account, no tracking, no server. Your progress and theme choice stay in this browser’s localStorage; nothing you type is sent anywhere. Clear site data to reset.
