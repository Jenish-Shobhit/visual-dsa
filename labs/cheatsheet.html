Complexity cheat sheet

Every structure and algorithm in the course, with the cost of each case. Colour follows the growth class of the dominant term, so the page reads like a heat map.

Reading the tables

  • n is the number of items; V and E are a graph’s vertices and edges; n and m are text and pattern lengths; L is a key’s length; k, b, d are range, base and digit count.
  • Amortized means averaged over a whole sequence of operations, so one call can be slow (a resize) while the total stays cheap. Average assumes typical input, or random choices made inside the algorithm. Worst holds for every input.
  • O(α(n)) is the inverse Ackermann function: below 5 for any n that fits in the universe, which is why it is coloured like O(1).
  • A stable sort keeps equal keys in their original order. Costs are the standard textbook ones; constant factors and cache behaviour decide many real races.
